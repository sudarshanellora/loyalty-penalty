# Draft post

One in four Irish mortgage holders pays over 4%. New customers pay 3.46%.

I analysed the Central Bank's mortgage rate data. Three things stood out:

1. About 180,000 households are above 4%. The gap to the new-customer rate costs them roughly €316m a year, around €1,750 each.

2. Almost nobody moves. 6,158 mortgages switched lender in the last year. That is under 1% of accounts.

3. Lenders have no commercial reason to fix it. On a simulated €7.9bn loan book, letting switchers leave costs €2.2m a year. Cutting rates for everyone above the market costs €12.7m.

The useful part for households: customers who renegotiate with their own lender average 3.23%, below what new customers pay. Ask your lender before you pay legal fees to switch.

I built a calculator so you can check your own number, and published the SQL, the Python and the assumptions: [link]

The €316m is a gap in rates, not money everyone can collect tomorrow. Some of these loans are inside a fixed term. Method and limits are in the write-up.
